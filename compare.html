iLock has no paid version, no subscription and no adverts. Two of the most-installed app lockers on Google Play pay their way with adverts and with purchases inside the app.

Going online

iLock does not have Android's internet permission, so it cannot send anything from your phone, and it holds no data about you anywhere else.

The data safety section of AppLock Pro's listing says the app may share location, app activity and two other kinds of data with third parties. The same section of Trusted Tools' listing says no data is shared with third parties, and that the app may collect app info and performance, and device or other IDs. If it matters to you, read each app's privacy policy before you give it this much access to your phone.

What iLock does not do

  • It has no vault for hiding photos and videos. Trusted Tools' listing describes one. In iLock you lock the gallery app instead.
  • It has no website blocker. AppLock Pro's listing describes one.
  • It cannot yet stay unlocked on a trusted Wi-Fi network. Trusted Tools' listing describes that. iLock has a time window and a 15-minute pause.
  • It has no alarm sound for wrong codes. AppLock Pro's listing describes one.
  • It has one lock-screen look that takes on each app's colour, with dark and light versions. Both other listings describe a choice of themes.
  • It offers a recovery code and a security question, not recovery by email, because it cannot go online.
  • It cannot check for updates by itself, for the same reason. A button opens the download page in your browser.
  • It needs a phone or tablet with Android 11 or later. There is no version for iPhone.
  • Like every app locker that is not part of Android itself, it can be removed by someone who knows your phone's own screen lock and how to use Safe mode.
