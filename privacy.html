Privacy policy

Last updated 16 November 2026. This policy covers the iLock app for Android (package uk.jj.ilock) and this website. iLock is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • iLock does not have Android's internet permission. It cannot send anything from your phone, to the developer or to anyone else.
  • iLock has no accounts and no servers. The developer receives nothing from your phone.
  • There is no advertising and no analytics in the app.
  • Everything iLock keeps is on your phone, in storage that other apps cannot read, and is deleted when you uninstall it.

What stays on your phone

iLock keeps the following in its private storage:

  • Your settings, including which apps are locked and the rule for each.
  • A scrambled, one-way version of your unlock code, your recovery code and your security answer. The code itself is never stored and cannot be read back.
  • The access log: the time of each unlock and each wrong code, and the app it was for. The log holds the latest 500 entries.
  • Intruder photos, if you have switched that feature on.
  • The sender and text of notifications from apps you have set to Mask or Hide, so that you can read them after unlocking. The log holds the latest 300.

None of this is included in Android's backup or in a transfer to a new phone.

iLock does not ask for the internet, your contacts, your location, your microphone, your messages or your files.

Services the app contacts

None. iLock has no internet permission and contacts nothing. When you tap Check for updates, iLock asks your browser to open this site's download page; what happens then is between your browser and GitHub, as described under This website.

Other people

The intruder photo may capture someone other than you, and the notification log may hold messages other people sent you. Both stay on your phone and are yours to manage. You are responsible for how you use them, and for telling people who share your phone that it is protected in this way if the law where you live expects that.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • The Activity screen has a Clear button for the access log, the intruder photos and the notification log, and each photo can be deleted singly.
  • Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling iLock deletes everything it holds. Your other apps and what is in them are not touched.
  • Nothing is held anywhere else, so there is nothing further to delete.

Your rights

UK and EU data protection law gives you rights over personal data that an organisation holds about you, including the right to see it, correct it and have it deleted.

The developer of iLock holds no personal data about you: there is no account, no server and no log of who uses the app. What iLock stores is on your own phone, where you control it directly using the steps above. If you need help, use the contact link below. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Security

The app's data is held in private storage that other apps cannot read. Your code is stored only as a salted, one-way hash. Because iLock cannot go online, there is no connection to intercept.

No app locker is proof against someone who knows your phone's own screen lock. The home page says plainly what iLock can and cannot do.

This website

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other websites. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub, as the host, receives the internet address of visitors and handles it under the GitHub Privacy Statement. The app file is downloaded from GitHub as well, on the same terms.

Children

iLock is a general-purpose privacy tool. It is not directed at children and its developer does not knowingly collect information from anyone.
